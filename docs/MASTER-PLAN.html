§2

Design rules for every season

  1. The idea is the mechanic. If a player can win while ignoring the idea, cut the scene. This rule stands from the game design.
  2. The villain's weapon is the lesson. Vane always uses the next idea against you.
  3. The forced yes (your idea). Each season removes one easy answer that an earlier season taught. The player must now handle the hard case with real numbers.
  4. One ramp per chapter (your idea). First: learn with help. Then: combine two ideas in one decision. Then: hold the ideas under regular checkpoints. Last: prove it, with real consequences for a wrong number. In Chapter 1 these are Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter; other chapters fit the ramp to their own seasons.
  5. The material sets the shape (decided 4 October). Four chapters, always. The number of seasons and each season's span of game time follow what must be taught. Real play time per season grows in later chapters.
  6. Load cap. At most 8 new core ideas per season and 6 named previews. Spring today has about 30 ideas; that is the main cause of the "too much talk" problem.
  7. Test only what was taught. A season may preview any idea. A check may only use ideas taught in an earlier season or earlier in the same season.
  8. Every earlier core idea comes back at least twice a season, inside play, not as a quiz.
  9. The valley is alive, and each change means something. A new house means more bread demand. Grey cloaks on the map mean Vane bought another note.
  10. Cutscenes for turning points only. At most 25 seconds. Skippable. Replayable from the journal. Each one shows a clue or a fact the player needs.

§9

Review of the game today

What works

  • One clear question pulls you in: a profitable farm, an empty chest.
  • The lesson is the plot. The villain's trap (a big order on slow terms) is the course's first lesson.
  • The Reeve's Court gives the season a real showdown, built from your own books.
  • Double-entry books under the hood: every statement ties out, so the numbers can be trusted.
  • Strong test coverage and bots that check balance (careful players survive; overtraders lose).

What holds it back

  • Too much at once. About 30 ideas in 2 to 3 hours. Up to five prompts compete in one day.
  • Vane is mostly talked about. He is on screen on days 12, 15, 24 and the end.
  • The world is still. The village looks the same on day 28 as on day 1.
  • Lessons and story still alternate in places. Where Maud sets a problem, the story stops.
  • The Court asked the same ten claims on a retake, and its break-even figure ignored your real wage bill. One practice problem taught time value the wrong way round. All three are fixed in v0.4.6.
  • Lessons are code. Each lesson is hand-written in the story file. Fifteen more seasons that way will not scale (phase 2).
  • Docs drift. Old names and old curriculum maps remain in some files. Once you approve, this plan replaces the old curriculum map and creative plan, and I mark those files superseded.
§10

Risks

  • Build cost. 16 seasons is large. Spring took about a week of heavy agent work. The season engine is the defence.
  • Overload creeps back. Every season will want more than 8 ideas. The cap holds unless you lift it.
  • Winter's kept books could frustrate. Weekly counts catch errors early; costs scale with the error; no single slip ends the game.
  • The curriculum moves. 94 of 168 sessions are unwritten. Each season starts by asking for its sessions.
  • Cutscene fatigue. Keep them short, skippable and useful.
  • What would change my mind on 4 seasons: if new players hold Spring's ideas a week later with no review, later chapters can use 2 or 3 seasons.

§14

Dev mode: test any moment without replaying

Not built yet, by your choice. This is the design, plus four cheap rules the platform keeps now so dev mode is easy later. Recommended timing: build it right after the platform phase, before Summer's build, because that is when replaying gets expensive.

The idea: rebuild any moment from data, in about a second

The game's economy is deterministic: same seed and same choices give the same books. The game already has bots that play whole seasons (a careful player, an overtrader, a reckless one) and recorded test playthroughs. So dev mode does not need saved games for every moment. It simulates to the moment you pick, instantly, and drops you there.

  1. Jump menu (behind ?dev=1, not linked anywhere): pick chapter → season → a day or a named story beat ("Vane arrives", "the Court", "the morning the notes are called") → how the player played until then (careful, overtrader, reckless, or "the full story run") → Go. You land just before the beat.
  2. State editor: change cash, stock, debts, trust per character and story flags, to test a branch ("Ashby at 3 hearts", "guarantee not found", "refused Vane").
  3. Chapter starts from presets: a later chapter reads only the hand-off record from the one before. Dev mode offers presets ("bought the mill, saved Ashby, Vane exposed"; "the canonical heir who lost"), so Chapter 2 can be tested without playing Chapter 1 at all.
  4. Snapshots and bug codes: "Save this moment" to a named slot, and every bug report carries a save code. You send the code; the builder opens your exact moment.
  5. Galleries: play any cutscene, scene or finale directly (today's ?court=1, generalised). Time controls: skip to the next event, sleep to day N.
  6. A screenshot gallery after every PR: CI renders every named beat and posts the pictures, so you can see a change without playing at all.
  7. Kept apart from real play: dev saves live in their own storage and earn no transcript credit.

The four rules the platform keeps now (cheap; they make all of the above possible)

  1. No unseeded randomness. Today there are two calls in game.js; the platform removes them.
  2. Everything about a moment lives in the save, so a moment can be written and read back.
  3. Every story beat, scene and cutscene has a stable id that the jump menu can list.
  4. The hand-off record between chapters has a schema, plus preset records for testing.

Size [Guessing]: 3 to 5 builder days once the platform phase is done. Most of it reuses the bots, the save format and the scene ids.
